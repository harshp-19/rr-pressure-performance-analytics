Methodology

Estimate win probability

Logistic regression estimates the batting side’s chance of winning before each ball from the match state, including runs needed, balls left, wickets in hand and run rates. Five match-grouped folds keep each match out of the training data used to predict it.

Identify high-leverage chases

A second model predicts the size of the win-probability swing using pre-ball information. The primary definition uses the top 20% of second-innings leverage and a 5–95% win-probability window. Chases have a known target and a better-performing model. First-innings leverage mainly flagged early uncertainty.

Score player performance

Situation-adjusted run value accounts for runs and wickets relative to expectations. The composite combines situational run value, leverage-weighted chase value, performance relative to ordinary balls, and the share of positive innings or spells.

Account for sample size

Small pressure samples are pulled toward the value predicted by the player’s ordinary-ball record. The other components shrink toward pool averages. Eligibility requires at least 50 pressure balls and 8 qualifying innings for batters, or 60 balls and 8 qualifying spells for bowlers. A qualifying innings or spell contains at least six pressure balls.

Resample matches

The audit reranked players across 400 match-level bootstrap samples. Top 10 frequency is the share of resamples in which the player ranked in the top 10. Ineligible resamples count as misses.

Test alternative definitions

The audit varied leverage cutoffs, innings, phase, competition, eligibility, weights, shrinkage and wicket credit. Selection changes across definitions, particularly with competition and phase restrictions.

Sensitivity to analysis choices

Top 10 overlap counts the original selections retained under each alternative. Rank correlation covers players eligible in both specifications.
